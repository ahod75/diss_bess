# 4. Results (~1,300 words; draft: ~1,036 prose + 2 tables)

## 4.1 Evaluation setup — ~100 words (draft: 160)
Purpose: state the comparison design before any result is reported.
- 4 forecasters (baseline, CRPS-only control, DFL single-price, DFL dual-price) × both
  settlement structures, via the recourse-aware robust formulation
  [@stratigakosPrescriptiveTreesIntegrated2022]
- Native vs. cross-evaluated: baseline/control price-agnostic (native under both); each
  DFL arm native under its own structure, cross-evaluated under the other
- N=5 seeds per retrained arm; baseline is a single fixed checkpoint, not seed-averaged
- Reporting convention fixed here: mean ± SD, range, win rate, sign consistency

## 4.2 Forecast behaviour — ~450 words (draft: 428)
Purpose: report the shape of what retraining changes, before explaining why (→ §5.2).
- Bias figure: shift is daytime-concentrated (hours 6–20, steepest 8–17), not uniform —
  flat/slightly above baseline at night
- Single-price closest to zero at 5/6 midday hours (11,13,14,15,16); control only wins
  narrowly at h12
- Non-monotonic: single-price dips negative at midday shoulders (11–12, 16), reverts
  positive through the core (13–15); dual-price dips-and-partially-rebounds
- Seed bands: single-price widest at every midday hour, dual-price tightest, control
  intermediate — several single-price hours straddle zero
- MAE figure + CRPS: diurnal accuracy pattern ~unchanged by retraining; CRPS is a
  checkpoint property, identical under either evaluation pipeline
- Forward-pointer to §5.2 for mechanism

## 4.3 Decision value — ~550 words (draft: 253 prose + 2 tables)
Purpose: the headline economic result, two tables, no explanation yet (→ §5.3/§5.4).
- **Table 4.1** (single-price settlement): baseline / control −0.104% / DFL single-price
  native −0.126% / DFL dual-price cross-eval −0.076%
- **Table 4.2** (dual-price settlement): baseline / control −0.096% / DFL dual-price
  native −0.045% / DFL single-price cross-eval −0.127%
- Claim 1: single-price-trained arm beats its control in both roles; dual-price-trained
  arm trails its control in both roles
- Claim 2: the two DFL rows land close together in both tables regardless of native/
  cross-eval status (→ §5.4)
- Claim 3: sign consistency splits cleanly by *which structure trained the forecaster*,
  not by native/cross-eval status — every single-price-touching row unanimous across 5
  seeds, every dual-price-touching row is not

## 4.4 Robustness — ~200 words (draft: 195)
Purpose: state the resolution limit and hedge the statistics honestly.
- Seed spread ≈ 0.05–0.10 pp sets the smallest distinguishable effect
- 4 one-sample t-tests reported (only ones computed); n=5 → individually weak; unanimous
  sign (from 4.3) treated as the stronger evidence
- 1-stage-vs-robust surrogate gap named as a stated limitation, not an RQ — moves to Ch.6
  if not built

# 5. Discussion (~2,200 words; draft: ~1,986)

## 5.1 What each settlement structure rewards — ~350 words (draft: 312)
Purpose: derive the theory, unconditionally, before checking it against data.
- Single-price: linear, corner-solution optimum, no pull toward accuracy
  [@stratigakosPrescriptiveTreesIntegrated2022]
- Dual-price: piecewise-linear hinge, genuine minimum at price-implied quantile
  τ = (π_up−π_da)/(π_up−π_down); GB data: τ=0.43 train / 0.50 test
- Grounded in real market design (§2.4), not an artefact of this study's formulation
- Ends with two testable predictions, handed directly to §5.2

## 5.2 Do the observed changes match? — ~450 words (draft: 424) — **Answers RQ2**
Purpose: test §5.1's predictions against §4.2's data.
- Dual-price: partial, non-monotonic convergence toward its minimum — real but imperfect
- Single-price: uneven correction + seed disagreement at every point — exactly what "no
  minimum" predicts, not noise
- Reconciles bias-shift-without-MAE-improvement → a location correction, not a sharper
  forecast
- Ties the same instability to §4.3's economic seed spread (single-price rows 0.085–0.089
  pp vs. dual-price rows 0.043–0.046 pp) — one mechanism, two datasets

## 5.3 Why decision value barely moved — ~450 words (draft: 410) — **Answers RQ1 (value)**
Purpose: explain 4.3's near-null economic result.
- Cameron refined: non-linearity necessary, not sufficient — what matters is whether the
  decision-optimal statistic differs from the statistical one [@cameronPerilsLearningOptimizing2021]
- Under GB dual-price, τ≈0.5 ⇒ it barely differs ⇒ dual-price DFL ≈ its control
- Single-price's small favourable movement is *not* counter-evidence: the combined
  training loss retains the pinball term throughout (self-balancing weighting), so
  single-price is never trained on the decision term alone — explains both the modest
  gain and why it isn't reliably signed

## 5.4 Transfer across settlement structures — ~250 words (draft: 170) — **Answers RQ1 (transfer)**
Purpose: extend 5.3's argument to the cross-eval numbers; paired with 5.3 under one RQ.
- Direction-independence: both cross-eval directions land close to their native
  counterparts (4.3's tables), both beneficial
- Follows from the same "targets aren't far apart" argument, not a separate mechanism
- Contrast with the expectation that DFL binds a forecaster rigidly to its training
  structure

## 5.5 Relation to prior DF results — ~350 words (draft: 276)
Purpose: situate the null/muted result against positive prior results, same problem class.
- Beichter (+9.45% cost reduction, PEFT) [@beichterDecisionfocusedFinetuningTime2025];
  Depoortere (+3.6%, worse RMSE, structurally similar cvxpylayers setup)
  [@depoortereDecisionfocusedLearningOptimal2026]; Werling (non-monotonic but favourable,
  point + probabilistic) [@werlingImpactForecastCharacteristics2023; @werlingPointProbabilisticForecasts2026]
- Structural contrast: all three use an abstract, stationary-geometry cost function;
  real settlement prices reverse sign period to period
- This asymmetry is only visible once the abstract cost is replaced with a real one

## 5.6 Wider implications — ~350 words (draft: 394)
Purpose: the governance point, reframed on the argument that still holds.
- Leads with the structural/algebraic fact (checkable before training), not the
  cross-transfer numbers (which no longer show harm)
- Empirical anchor = seed-variance evidence (still valid), not cross-price harm (reversed)
- "Bidding strategy in a forecast's clothing" — arrested only by whatever calibration
  term happens to still be in the loss, grounded in §2.4's real-market framing
- Closes with two concrete, checkable conditions an operator can apply *before* training:
  (1) does the settlement's decision objective have a genuine minimum? (2) if not, does
  the training objective retain an independent calibration-anchoring term?
